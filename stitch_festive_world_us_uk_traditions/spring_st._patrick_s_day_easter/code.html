🇺🇸 US Cultural Celebration
March 17th

St. Patrick's Day

Irish-American heritage celebrated in giant urban spectacles.

Chicago River Dyed Emerald Green

verified Cultural Highlights

1
Massive Street Parades

The New York City and Boston parades attract millions of spectators with marching bands, bagpipes, and honored veteran groups.

2
The Green River in Chicago

The city of Chicago uses eco-friendly dye to turn the entire Chicago River vivid green for the festival weekend, a spectacle watched by thousands from bridges.

3
Wearing Green & Shamrocks

Tradition dictates that you wear green clothing or risk being playfully pinched by peers; people pin clover shamrocks to their jackets for good luck.

4
Traditional Irish-American Food

Families and pubs serve slow-cooked corned beef with cabbage and soda bread, accompanied by bright green frosted cupcakes and specialty festive beverages.

school Classroom Language Box (B1) Active Syntax
“People of all backgrounds wear green and celebrate Irish culture on March 17th in major American cities.”
Key Term: Shamrock — a three-leaf clover plant recognized internationally as the national symbol of Ireland and Irish heritage.
🇺🇸 & 🇬🇧 Shared Anglo-American Tradition
Spring Sunday

Easter Traditions

Chocolate treats, festive garden hunts, and seasonal rebirth.

Garden Egg Hunt & Spring Florals

verified Cultural Highlights

1
Outdoor Easter Egg Hunts

Parents hide colorful plastic eggs (filled with sweets) or hand-painted eggs throughout gardens and public parks for excited children to discover.

2
Chocolate Eggs & Bunnies

In the UK, shops display giant hollow chocolate eggs wrapped in shiny foil. In the US, hollow chocolate bunnies and colorful marshmallow ‘Peeps’ are holiday favorites.

3
The White House Egg Roll (US)

A famous 140-year-old presidential tradition on Easter Monday where young students roll colorful dyed hard-boiled eggs with long wooden spoons across the South Lawn.

4
Hot Cross Buns & Sunday Roast (UK)

British bakeries produce warm, spiced fruit buns with a baked icing cross on Good Friday, followed by roasted spring lamb, mint sauce, and potatoes on Easter Sunday.

school Classroom Language Box (B1) Descriptive Lexis
“Children carry small wicker baskets while searching for hidden chocolate eggs in the garden.”
Key Term: Egg hunt — an interactive outdoor game where children search for hidden chocolate, dyed, or surprise-filled eggs representing new life.
Interactive Whiteboard Activity

Classroom Presentation Discussion

“Why do you think cities celebrate cultural holidays from other countries, like St. Patrick's Day in the US?”

psychology B1 Grammar Clinic Purpose Clauses

Expressing Purpose: ‘In order to’ & ‘So that’

Use these connectors to explain the intention behind spring traditions:

1. In order to + verb:
“Cities organize parades in order to celebrate cultural diversity.”
2. So that + subject + can/will:
“Parents hide eggs so that children can explore the garden outdoors.”
