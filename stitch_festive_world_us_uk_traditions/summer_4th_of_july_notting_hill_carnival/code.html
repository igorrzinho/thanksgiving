Core Cultural Customs

1. Family Barbecues & Cookouts

Neighbors gather in backyards and parks to grill hamburgers, hot dogs, ribs, and sweet corn in casual community lunches.

2. Patriotic Colors

Everyone wears red, white, and blue shirts, hats, and festive face paint to express national unity and pride.

Core Cultural Customs
public

1. Celebration of British Caribbean Culture

Europe’s biggest street festival, honouring Caribbean music, dance traditions, and multicultural London heritage since 1966.

theater_comedy

2. Masquerade & Costumes (‘Mas’)

Dancers parade in breathtaking bespoke costumes with enormous plumes of dyed feathers, sparkling gems, and glittering wings.

speaker

3. Sound Systems & Steel Pan Bands

Towering stationary street sound systems blast reggae, soca, and calypso rhythms while hundreds of thousands dance freely.

restaurant

4. Street Food Delights

Lively stalls serve authentic Jamaican jerk chicken, fried sweet plantains, warm meat patties, and fresh coconut water.

Oral Practice

Encourage learners to frame their thoughts using these intermediate conversational connectors:

Sharing Personal View “In my view...” e.g. “In my view, street carnivals create greater cultural awareness.”
Highlighting Novelty “What surprises me most is...” e.g. “What surprises me most is the size of the sound systems.”
Direct Contrast “Unlike the US holiday, the UK event...” e.g. “Unlike the 4th of July, carnival centers around Caribbean dance.”
Finding Common Ground “Both traditions demonstrate that...” e.g. “Both traditions demonstrate that food and music bring people together.”
